Chain setup

A chain that drops pixels near its far end while the page is loading is not short of volts — it is being interrupted mid-frame. Lowering the refresh rate is the cure and the test at once: fewer frames means fewer windows in which a WiFi interrupt can land inside one. 60 Hz is the default; 30 is still perfectly smooth.

Run Wiring test: the field must cycle red → green → blue → white in that order. If two of them are swapped, pick the matching colour order above — the change is live, no reflash.

Which machine

Put a different machine on this wall

Two files: the playfield plan and the attract capture. Download them for your title from pinballs.store, upload both, and the wall reboots on your machine. Your pixel placements are kept.

A different machine has different inserts, so you will need to place the pixels again afterwards.

Network

Connected to – · signal – at the wall. Below −75 dBm, expect the page to feel slow and updates to fail.

Password

Anyone on your network can otherwise change this wall, update its firmware or wipe it. Setting a password closes everything, including this page. Forgotten it? Hold the three buttons on the board for five seconds — the wall reddens as you hold, blinks three times, and comes back factory-fresh.

Pick a network above to fill this in. The wall reboots to join, then answers at http://playfield-<id>.local/ on that network.

The wall gets its network from the Home app when you pair it — there is nothing to type here. To move it to another network, use Forget all homes & re-pair in the Siri section and pair it again on the new one.

Other walls

Each wall announces itself on your network, so they can act as one. Detection always runs — choosing a behaviour below is what makes them respond to each other.

Siri & voice assistants

The wall joins Apple Home, Google Home or Alexa as a Matter accessory: one master lamp, plus one switch per mode. Rename each switch in your home app — whatever you type there is the phrase the assistant listens for.

Reset

Four levels, from harmless to final. They are separate because they answer different questions — “I don't like this setting” is not the same as “I'm giving this wall away”. All four are also on the wall's own screen, under Reset.

Restart keeps everything. Reset look puts colours and levels back to factory, and deliberately leaves the LED count, order and power budget alone — those describe how this wall is built, and wiping them turns a working wall into a mis-driven one. Forget homes unpairs it from Siri and drops the network with it, since pairing is what provides it. Erase everything does all of that plus the wiring and the mapping.
